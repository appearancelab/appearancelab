Remote 2020 (remote2020.org)

Remote 2020 is a tool for running remote, web-based experiments.

  • Stimuli and responses are transmitted entirely over the web.
  • Experimenter and participant are connected in real time.
  • No registration or server setup is required.

The platform also serves as an integrated environment for teaching experimental programming:

  • An integrated debugging window helps students learn programming through experiments.
  • A dedicated classroom mode allows the instructor’s screen to be visible on all students’ screens.

Pixel-Grids

Our Pixel-grids method captures the appearance of stimuli that are created on different types of grids. Observers respond on a grid that has the same structure as the stimulus, but can differ in size, allowing highly precise analyses of appearance.

Participants select squares on the response grid so that the pattern matches the appearance of a stimulus that is otherwise hard to see. The response grid can be used while the stimulus is presented or after stimulus offset. In the original demonstrations, a comparison stimulus on the right shows the captured appearance at the same size as the presented stimulus.

We currently use Pixel-grids to investigate the appearance of stimuli presented to the retina using adaptive optics in combination with scanning laser ophthalmoscopy (AOSLO).

Multi-dimensional Method of Adjustment

The Multi-dimensional Method of Adjustment (MMA) is an interface that allows observers to adjust stimuli along several appearance dimensions at once. It offers multiple degrees of freedom to capture stimulus appearance and to create shape metamers.

We combine the method with eye tracking and gaze-contingent stimulus presentation to prevent eye movements to peripheral stimuli while still allowing long presentation times and repeated observations. Using familiar touchpad gestures, observers change the number, position, orientation, and length of segments in a probe stimulus until it matches the target’s appearance.

  • Sayim, B., Melnik, N., Yildirim, F. Z., & Coates, D. R. (2017). Creating peripheral shape metamers.
  • Coates, D. R., Yildirim, F. Z., Melnik, N., & Sayim, B. (2017). Deconstructing peripheral appearance.

Geometrically Restricted Image Descriptors (GRIDs)

The GRIDs method captures shape appearance with higher detail than standard categorical response tasks. Stimuli consist of line elements placed on a grid by connecting grid nodes. Each line is treated as a discrete target, and observers reproduce target appearance by placing lines on a freely viewed response grid.

Lines are defined as strokes connecting two nodes, while segments connect adjacent nodes without passing through any other dot. High-speed eye tracking allows gaze-contingent presentation of these shapes in the visual periphery.

  • Melnik, N., Coates, D. R., & Sayim, B. (2021). Geometrically Restricted Image Descriptors: A method to capture the appearance of shape. [open access]

Gaze-contingent Free Capture

In the Free Capture method, participants draw how a stimulus appears to them without any predefined constraints. High-speed eye tracking and gaze-contingent presentation provide precise control over retinal location: the stimulus is only shown when the observer fixates a central target.

Using Free Capture, we examined peripheral crowding and characterised how crowding alters stimulus appearance. With this method, we discovered the phenomenon of redundancy masking, where the number of perceived elements is reduced relative to the number presented. Demonstrations compare free viewing with gaze-contingent presentations of complex figures, such as the Rey–Osterrieth Complex Figure.

  • Sayim, B., & Taylor, H. (2019). Letters lost: Capturing appearance in crowded peripheral vision reveals a new kind of masking. [download] [online]
  • Coates, D. R., Wagemans, J., & Sayim, B. (2017). Diagnosing the visual periphery: Using the Rey–Osterrieth Complex Figure test to evaluate peripheral visual function. [open access]
